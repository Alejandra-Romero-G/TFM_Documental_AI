- Heat stress is a workplace hazard caused by exertion or hot environments, leading to conditions such as heat stroke, heat exhaustion, and heat cramps. 
- Risks include heat stroke characterized by inability to regulate body temperature, heat exhaustion due to excessive loss of water and salt, and heat cramps affecting those who sweat excessively. 
- First aid for heat stroke involves requesting immediate medical assistance, moving the worker to a cool area, removing excess clothing, and applying cool water to the body. For heat exhaustion, rest in a cool area, drink fluids, and take a cool bath. 
- Preventive measures include avoiding heavy exertion, extreme heat, sun exposure, and high humidity; monitoring physical condition and signs of heat illness; wearing light-colored, loose-fitting, breathable clothing; gradually building up to heavy work; scheduling work during cooler times; taking frequent breaks; drinking water regularly; and being aware of protective clothing increasing risks.
